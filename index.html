Which colleges survive every ranking lens — not just one.

Five major global ranking systems disagree constantly, because each rewards a different definition of excellence. This report finds the US universities that place consistently across all five — QS, THE, ARWU, CWUR, and US News — at once. Choose a cutoff below to see how the qualifying set changes.

Why the systems disagree

Five instruments measuring five different things

None of these rankings measure "quality" directly — they measure proxies, and the proxies aren't the same. A school can dominate one system's proxy and barely register on another's.

QS

Reputation-weighted

Nearly half the score comes from academic and employer reputation surveys — perception, not just output.

THE

Blended profile

Splits weight across teaching, research environment, research quality, and industry income — the most balanced of the five.

ARWU

Pure research output

Nobel and Fields winners, highly-cited researchers, Nature/Science papers. Zero reputation surveys, zero self-reported data.

CWUR

Outcomes-heavy

Weighs alumni employment and faculty quality alongside research — rewards institutions that convert prestige into placement.

US News

Citation impact

Built almost entirely from bibliometric data — publication volume and citation rate carry most of the weight.

The approach

What "consensus" means here

The filter: take a school's rank in each of the five systems' most recent edition, and keep only the schools that place 25th or better in every single one. Excelling in three out of five doesn't count — a school with a brilliant QS reputation score and a mediocre research-output score is still excluded.

The score: for the schools that clear the bar, rank them by the simple average of their five positions — a lower number means the school is consistently near the very top, not just consistently qualifying.

consensus_score = ( US News + THE + QS + CWUR + ARWU ) / 5

This rewards robustness over peak performance. A school that's 1st in one system and 60th in another never qualifies, no matter how impressive that first-place finish looks in isolation.
